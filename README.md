# JOMT – Just One More Texture

A comprehensive Texture Generator powered by pure, deterministic math, with a lot of control for developers, designers, artists and other creative individuals.

**License:** code under [MIT](LICENSE) · textures you generate are [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) (free for any use, no attribution).

JOMT runs in the browser: it needs no build step or server: open `index.html` in Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

## Usage
- Every generator has a **preset menu** at the top of its panel (20–25 looks, grouped into Realistic, Stylized, Game-inspired and Sci-fi). Pick one or step through them with ‹ ›; applying a preset can be undone, and changing any setting turns it into custom settings. Generators define them as `presets: [{ name, group, values, aspect? }]`; a **Default** entry (the generator's own defaults) is added at the top of every list automatically.
- Effect textures that are usually tinted in the engine (muzzle flashes, cookie, lightning, light cone, particle) default to white / grayscale and all their presets are uncolored 1:1 textures. Their color fields are still there if you want to bake a tint in. Material and particle generators (grass, billboards, flashes, lightning, light cone) use 1:1 presets; only UI generators (UI border, shape buttons) keep wide presets.
- Generators carry up to 3 **tags** (Horror, PBR, Texturing, UI, Shooter, Military, VFX, Lighting, Nature, Decals, Vehicles, Sci-fi). The filter bar above the generator grid (start page and the **+** picker) shows only the generators with the selected tag (one tag at a time; click it again or use **Reset filter** to show all). The filter is remembered. Generators define them as `tags: [...]`; the tag order lives in `U.TAGS` (core.js).
- **Search** (right end of the filter bar on the start page and in the **+** picker; Ctrl+K or / jumps to it): fuzzy search over generator names (English and the current language), tags, descriptions, use cases and hidden keywords (`U.KEYWORDS` in `js/search.js`: synonyms and things a generator makes, e.g. "earth" → Planet, "jet" → Planes & Jets). Matches by exact word, word prefix ("plane" → Planet, Planes & Jets), substring, or with a typo or two in names, tags and keywords ("brik" → Tiles & Bricks); results are sorted by relevance and combine with the tag filter. Enter opens the best match, ↓ moves into the results, Esc clears; the × next to the tags resets tag and search.
- Generator cards get a subtle hover: a slight tilt toward the pointer and a pure white specular streak that slides with the rotation. An open generator explains itself in the panel header with its primary intended use and, where it applies, a secondary one; the ⓘ button hides or shows that box per generator (remembered). The **inspector size** menu in the top bar (90–175 %) scales the whole settings panel for big screens and is remembered in the browser (`uses: [primary, secondary?]` in `U.register`; texts and their translations in `js/i18n/generators.js`).
- Use **+** (or press `N`) to open a new generator tab. Double-click a tab name to rename it. Middle-click closes a tab.
- The preview renders at 256, 512 or 1024 px. Export goes from 256 up to 8K as PNG, WebP or JPG, or you can copy it to the clipboard.
- Undo / redo per tab: `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Y` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or the ↶ ↷ buttons (a whole slider drag is one step).
- **Aspect ratio:** pick a preset (1:1, 2:1, 16:9, …) or **Custom…** in the Aspect menu (top left) and type any width : height ratio (e.g. 3 : 1.25, sides 0.01–100); ⇄ swaps width and height. The export size sets the long side.
- Export sizes go down to 16×16; up to 128 px a **Hard pixels** toggle gives crisp pixel-art output, and the Preview menu can show the exact export size.
- **Refraction preview** (Glass & Refraction Normals, Water Normals): a live WebGL view of a scene seen through the normal map fills the viewport; the texture itself is a thumbnail in the corner panel next to the preview settings. ⇄ swaps back to a small preview box over the texture (remembered).
- **Normal map mode** (all PBR and Texturing generators except Grime / Grunge; a generator opts out with `normalMap: false`): the Texture / Normal map switch at the top of the panel (or `M`) shows a tangent-space normal map derived from the texture instead of the texture. The texture settings are replaced by a single **Normal** tab: strength, smoothing, Sobel or central-difference filter, height source (brightness, R, G, B, alpha), invert, height contrast, OpenGL / DirectX green channel and edge wrapping (on for tileable generators). Export and Copy then save the normal map (file name ends in `_normal`).
- **Smoothness mode** (PBR, Texturing and Decals generators; opt out with `smoothMap: false`, opt in with `smoothMap: true`): the Texture | Normal map | Smoothness switch turns the texture into a grayscale smoothness map. Source (brightness, R, G, B, alpha / coverage, or the generator's own height map), invert source, smoothness range (one min … max slider), curve, smoothing, transparency (fill with a value, or keep the texture's alpha for decals). **Output roughness** inverts the map for engines that expect roughness (Unreal, glTF, Godot, Blender) instead of smoothness (Unity). Every generator starts from a suggested range for its material (e.g. brick 0.08–0.35, water 0.85–0.98, fresh blood 0.55–0.92), shown in the panel header and restorable with **Use suggestion**. Files end in `_smoothness` or `_roughness`. See `js/smooth.js`.
- **Metallic mode** (PBR generators, plus generators with a metal suggestion such as Bullet, Fence, Scratches, Weapon Skin, Impact, Artifact Surfaces, Grime and the mask generators; opt out with `metalMap: false`, opt in with `metalMap: true`): the Metallic switch turns the texture into a grayscale metallic map (white = metal). Same controls as the smoothness map: source, invert, metallic range, curve, smoothing, transparency. Every generator starts from a suggested range with a note in the panel header (non-metals such as stone, wood, soil or paint suggest 0–0, so they export black; brass cases 0.9–1 from the coverage; scratches through paint 0–1); **Use suggestion** restores it. Files end in `_metallic`. See `js/metal.js`.
- **Ambient light:** every generator with a light direction (Sand, Gravel, Mud, Rock, Debris, Impact, Maggots, Leafy Branch, Blood Pool, Bullet, Cursor, Marker, Clouds, Planet) has a **Light** select: *Angled* (the light angle) or *Ambient* (soft light from all around, no direction, no cast shadows or hot-spot highlights; slopes darken evenly and cavities keep their occlusion). Use Ambient for textures that the engine lights itself.
- **Saturation and Colors** (in the **Output** tab of every generator, joined to the generator's own Output section; global for all tabs, remembered, no undo step): *Saturation* (0–200 %, 0 % = grayscale) applies to the preview and every color export, not to normal / smoothness / metallic maps. *Colors* reduces the preview and every export to at most N colors (2–256; median-cut palette, posterized without dithering, alpha untouched); at the right end (*All*) it is off. Semi-transparent pixels can drift by a value or two because the browser canvas stores premultiplied alpha. See `js/post.js`.
- **Tilesheet mode** (both muzzle flash generators, Particle, Hit Marker): the Single frame / Tilesheet switch (or `M`) renders the flash forming and fading as a 2×2 up to 8×8 animation sheet, frames left to right, top to bottom. A **Tilesheet** settings tab sets the grid and the lifecycle (build-up, hold, start / end size, fade curve, dissipation, flicker, new noise per frame); the flash settings stay editable next to it. A player in the corner of the viewport loops the frames at the chosen FPS. The export size is the whole sheet (e.g. 2K at 4×4 = 512 px frames); the file name ends in `_sheet4x4`. Particle has its own *Flipbook* tab for the sheet: spin over the animation (with per-layer variation), noise evolution (direction), boil while fading, rise and how strongly the start / end size scales the layers. Other generators can opt in with an `animate(params, frame)` hook (see `js/sheet.js`); with `animLoop: true` they get a seamless loop instead of the build-up / fade lifecycle (used by Insects & Spiders). A generator with both a normal map and an animation shows all three modes: Texture | Normal map | Tilesheet, and `M` cycles through them.
- **Separate layer export** (Linear, Radial and Shape Bar; *Output* tab; the Scope has its own lens / reticle / emissive split): Download saves several pixel-aligned PNGs instead of one: `_bg` (shadow + empty bar + border), `_fill` (the fill, at 100 % unless *Fill layer at 100 %* is off), optionally `_frame` (*Border as its own top layer*, stacked above the fill) and `_progress` (*Progress map layer*: the fill shape at 100 %, gray = position along the fill from 0 at the start to 1 at the end; in the engine show the fill where gray ≤ health, which gives correct linear, centered and radial fills from one texture). Noise, distortion and grunge are identical on every layer, so they stack exactly. *Preview layer* shows any single layer. Turn *Separate layer export* off for one combined image at the preview fill amount.
- **WebGL refraction preview** (Glass & Refraction Normals, Water Normals): a box in the corner of the viewport shows a background (pool tiles, landscape, text, checker, stripes or a UI panel) seen through the current normal map, with distortion, tiling, highlight, chromatic split and tint; for water, *Animate* scrolls two copies against each other. The settings (⚙ folds them away) are preview-only and remembered in the browser. A generator opts in with `glPreview: { isNormal(p), flipY(p), water? }`; when its preview shows something else (e.g. the height map), the app renders the normal map separately with `{ glNormal: true }` (see `js/glpreview.js`).
- Shortcuts: `T` toggles the 3×3 Tiling Preview, `R` picks a new seed, `M` toggles normal map / tilesheet mode, `F` fits the view, `1` shows actual preview pixels, `Ctrl+S` exports.
- **Languages:** English, Deutsch, Français, Español and Italiano, switched with the flags in the top right corner (the choice is remembered in localStorage). Everything is translated: interface, generator names and descriptions, every setting, tooltip and option, and all presets. Translations live in `js/i18n/<lang>.js` as `UGTG.addLang(code, { "English text": "Translation" })`; anything missing falls back to English, and code uses `UGTG.t("English text", ...args)` with `{0}` placeholders.
- Tabs and settings are saved in the browser's localStorage.
- `index.html#new=camo` (or another generator id) opens straight into a new tab.

## Adding a generator
Create `js/gen/<name>.js` that calls `UGTG.register({ id, name, desc, icon, params, render(ctx, W, H, p) })` and add a script tag for it to `index.html`. The settings panel is built automatically from `params`. Write sizes relative to `W`/`H` so the preview and every export size look the same.

- **Own map sources:** `mapSources: [[id, label]]` adds sources to the smoothness / metallic Source menu; the app then renders `render(ctx, W, H, p, { source: id })` as a grayscale image to drive the map (Moss uses it for its leaf tips).
- **Range sliders:** a param `{ key, key2, type: 'span', min, max, step, default, default2, cross? }` shows one slider with two handles for a min … max pair; it writes `key` (low end) and `key2` (high end), so render code and presets keep using both keys. Without `cross: true` the handles push each other so low ≤ high. Every min / max setting uses it.
- **Action buttons:** a param `{ type: 'button', buttons: [{ label, run(values), tip?, icon?: 'dice', primary? }] }` shows a row of buttons; `run` edits the values and the panel rebuilds (used by the symbol randomizer).
- **Separate layer export:** `exportLayers(params)` returns `[{ id, suffix }]` (or `null` for one image). Export then renders once per layer with `render(ctx, W, H, p, { layer: id })` and downloads `<name>_<suffix>_<W>x<H>.png` for each; the Download button shows the file count. Copy always takes the combined image.
- **Frame sequences:** export layers with ids `frame0`, `frame1` … are treated as an animation: the corner player renders and loops them at `params.fps` (or 12 fps). Used by Loading Icon, Damage Vignette and Maggots.
- **Shared wear stack** (`js/wear.js`): `U.wearParams()` adds the Noise + Grunge sections, `U.wearFields(W, H, p, S, silhouette?)` precomputes the noise once per render (on a fixed grid per short side, so preview = export), `U.wearApply(canvas, p, fields, { grunge, color, t })` warps, tints and erodes a canvas. `js/bar.js` builds the health bar pipeline on top of it from a small geometry object.
- **Shared ground lighting** (`js/ground.js`, used by Sand, Gravel and Mud): `U.groundLightParams(defaults)` adds the Lighting section (style, light: angled or ambient, light angle / height, relief, cast shadows, ambient occlusion, wetness, output); a generator writes its albedo into an ImageData and its height (short-side units) into a Float32Array, then calls `U.groundFinish(ctx, img, height, W, H, p, { flags, wetMap, aoScale, heightRef, shadowLen })` for the lit color, flat albedo or height map.
